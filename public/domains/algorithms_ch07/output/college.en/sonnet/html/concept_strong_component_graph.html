Strong Component Graph

Definition. Let $G = (V, E)$ be a directed graph. A strongly connected component (SCC) of $G$ is a maximal subset $S \subseteq V$ such that for every ordered pair $(u, v)$ with $u, v \in S$, there exists a directed path from $u$ to $v$ and a directed path from $v$ to $u$. The strong component graph (also called the condensation of $G$), denoted $G^{\mathrm{SCC}}$, is the directed graph whose vertices are the SCCs of $G$ and whose edges are defined by

$$ [S_i] \to [S_j] \in E(G^{\mathrm{SCC}}) \iff i \neq j \text{ and } \exists\, u \in S_i,\, v \in S_j \text{ such that } (u,v) \in E. $$

A fundamental consequence of maximality is that $G^{\mathrm{SCC}}$ contains no directed cycle; that is, $G^{\mathrm{SCC}}$ is always a directed acyclic graph (DAG).

Worked Example. Consider the graph $G$ with vertices $\{1,2,3,4,5\}$ and edges

$$ 1\!\to\!2,\quad 2\!\to\!3,\quad 3\!\to\!1,\quad 3\!\to\!4,\quad 4\!\to\!5,\quad 5\!\to\!4. $$

Applying Kosaraju's algorithm (two depth-first search passes, the second on the transpose $G^T$) identifies the SCCs as $S_1 = \{1,2,3\}$, $S_2 = \{4,5\}$. The condensation $G^{\mathrm{SCC}}$ has two nodes with the single edge $[S_1]\to[S_2]$, confirming it is a DAG.

Key Theorem (Uniqueness of the Condensation). Every directed graph $G$ has a unique condensation $G^{\mathrm{SCC}}$, and $G^{\mathrm{SCC}}$ is a DAG. Proof sketch: Uniqueness follows because the SCCs partition $V$ (reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity of mutual reachability constitute an equivalence relation). Acyclicity follows by contradiction: a directed cycle among distinct SCCs $[S_i]\to\cdots\to[S_i]$ would imply all vertices in those components are mutually reachable, contradicting their maximality as separate SCCs. $\square$

The condensation is practically significant: any algorithm that requires acyclicity — topological sort, dynamic programming on graphs, reachability queries — can be applied to $G^{\mathrm{SCC}}$ even when $G$ itself contains cycles.

Lab Cell.

import sympy as sp
import networkx as nx

G = nx.DiGraph()
G.add_edges_from([(1,2),(2,3),(3,1),(3,4),(4,5),(5,4)])

sccs = list(nx.strongly_connected_components(G))
condensation = nx.condensation(G, scc=sccs)

print("SCCs:", sccs)
print("Condensation edges:", list(condensation.edges()))
print("Is DAG:", nx.is_directed_acyclic_graph(condensation))

Running this cell produces SCCs: [{1,2,3}, {4,5}], one condensation edge, and confirms the DAG property, matching the hand computation above.
