Tower Of Hanoi

Definition. The Tower of Hanoi is a combinatorial puzzle consisting of three pegs—conventionally labeled $A$, $B$, and $C$—and $n$ disks of strictly distinct diameters, initially stacked in decreasing order of size on peg $A$. A move transfers the topmost disk of one peg to another peg, subject to the Hanoi constraint: no disk may rest on a smaller disk. The objective is to relocate the entire stack from peg $A$ to peg $C$. Let $H(n)$ denote the minimum number of moves required to solve the $n$-disk puzzle.

Worked Example ($n = 3$). Label the disks $d_1 < d_2 < d_3$ by increasing diameter. The optimal sequence proceeds in $7$ moves:

$$d_1: A\to C,\quad d_2: A\to B,\quad d_1: C\to B,\quad d_3: A\to C,\quad d_1: B\to A,\quad d_2: B\to C,\quad d_1: A\to C.$$

At each stage the constraint is satisfied, and no shorter solution exists, as verified below.

Key Theorem. The minimum number of moves satisfying the Hanoi constraint for $n$ disks is

$$H(n) = 2^n - 1.$$

Proof sketch (by strong induction). Base case: $H(1) = 1 = 2^1 - 1$. For the inductive step, assume $H(k) = 2^k - 1$ for all $k < n$. To move the largest disk $d_n$ from $A$ to $C$, one must first clear $d_1,\ldots,d_{n-1}$ onto $B$ (requiring at least $H(n-1)$ moves), then move $d_n$ in exactly $1$ move, then transfer the $n-1$ disks from $B$ to $C$ (requiring at least $H(n-1)$ moves again). Hence

$$H(n) \geq 2\,H(n-1) + 1.$$

The recursive algorithm achieves this bound, so $H(n) = 2\,H(n-1) + 1$, whose closed form resolves to $H(n) = 2^n - 1$ by unrolling the recurrence or solving the linear recurrence relation $H(n) - 2H(n-1) = 1$ with $H(0)=0$. $\blacksquare$

The recurrence also implies an exponential lower bound: solving the puzzle for $n = 64$ disks (the legendary Brahma temple problem) requires $2^{64}-1 \approx 1.8 \times 10^{19}$ moves.

Lab Cell (SymPy).

from sympy import symbols, Function, rsolve

n = symbols('n', positive=True, integer=True)
H = Function('H')

# Solve H(n) = 2*H(n-1) + 1, H(0) = 0
recurrence = H(n) - 2*H(n-1) - 1
solution = rsolve(recurrence, H(n), {H(0): 0})
print("Closed form:", solution)          # Expected: 2**n - 1

# Verify for small values
for k in range(1, 8):
    print(f"H({k}) = {solution.subs(n, k)}")
