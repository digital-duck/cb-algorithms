Concept Book: Tower Of Hanoi

Algorithms Ch02 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

The Tower of Hanoi is more than a childhood puzzle; it is a precise laboratory for the most fundamental ideas in discrete mathematics and algorithm analysis. Having built up the machinery of mathematical induction, recurrence relations, and exponential growth through the preceding chapters, we arrive at a single problem that exercises every tool simultaneously — and whose solution is provably optimal.

What the problem achieves. The puzzle consists of $n$ disks of strictly decreasing size stacked on a source peg $A$, with two auxiliary pegs $B$ and $C$. The goal is to transfer all $n$ disks to peg $C$, moving exactly one disk per step and never placing a larger disk atop a smaller one. Let $T(n)$ denote the minimum number of moves required.

Worked example. For $n = 3$: move the top two disks from $A$ to $B$ (requiring $T(2)$ moves), move the largest disk from $A$ to $C$ (one move), then move the two disks from $B$ to $C$ (requiring $T(2)$ moves again). This gives $T(3) = 2T(2) + 1 = 2(3) + 1 = 7$.

Key theorem. The recurrence $T(n) = 2T(n-1) + 1$ with $T(1) = 1$ has the closed-form solution

$$T(n) = 2^n - 1.$$

Proof sketch. By induction: the base case $T(1) = 2^1 - 1 = 1$ holds trivially. Assuming $T(k) = 2^k - 1$, substituting into the recurrence yields $T(k+1) = 2(2^k - 1) + 1 = 2^{k+1} - 1$. The same argument establishes that no algorithm can do better, because the largest disk must move exactly once, and the $n-1$ smaller disks must be cleared twice — once before and once after — giving the lower bound $T(n) \geq 2T(n-1) + 1$. Optimality follows.

This result is the natural endpoint of the book because it unifies every major theme: induction provides the proof, the recurrence encodes recursive structure, and the closed form reveals exponential complexity — a concrete warning that problems with innocent-looking sizes can demand astronomically many steps.

Lab cell.

from sympy import symbols, rsolve, Function

n = symbols('n', positive=True, integer=True)
T = Function('T')
solution = rsolve(T(n) - 2*T(n-1) - 1, T(n), {T(1): 1})
print(solution)   # expects 2**n - 1
