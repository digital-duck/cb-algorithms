Concept Book: Irrational Capacities Nontermination

Algorithms Ch11 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

Throughout this book, each concept has sharpened a single instrument: the ability to reason precisely about limits, measurement, and the behavior of infinite processes. The capstone concept, irrational capacities nontermination, forges those instruments into a unified lens. It asserts, in essence, that whenever the capacity of a system—its information content, its geometric measure, or its expressive power—is an irrational number, the process that generates or characterizes that capacity cannot terminate in finitely many steps. Irrationality and nontermination are not accidents that coexist; they are two faces of the same mathematical phenomenon.

Definition. Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a capacity functional assigning a non-negative real value $\mathcal{C}(S)$ to a system $S$ drawn from a class $\mathcal{S}$. We say $S$ exhibits irrational-capacity nontermination if $\mathcal{C}(S) \in \mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}$ and every algorithmic procedure $\mathcal{A}$ that attempts to decide membership in $S$—or to compute $\mathcal{C}(S)$ exactly—fails to halt on at least one admissible input.

The canonical example is Chaitin's halting probability,

$$\Omega = \sum_{\text{program } p \text{ halts}} 2^{-|p|},$$

where $|p|$ denotes the length in bits of program $p$ under a universal prefix-free Turing machine. One verifies that $\Omega \in (0,1)$ and that $\Omega$ is not only irrational but transcendental and algorithmically random: no finite prefix of its binary expansion can be extended by any computable rule. Knowing the first $n$ bits of $\Omega$ allows one to decide the halting problem for all programs of length at most $n$, yet no Turing machine can output those $n$ bits for arbitrary $n$. The capacity of the halting set, measured by $\Omega$, is irrevocably irrational, and the decision process for that set is irrevocably nonterminating.

Worked example. Consider the continued-fraction expansion of $\sqrt{2} = [1; 2, 2, 2, \ldots]$. Define a one-tape Turing machine $M_{\sqrt{2}}$ that outputs successive convergents $p_k/q_k \to \sqrt{2}$. At every step $k$, the machine appends another partial quotient $2$ and continues; it never enters a halting state. Meanwhile, the capacity of the sequence of rational approximations—measured as the Hausdorff content of the limit point $\{\sqrt{2}\}$ relative to the covering radii $|p_k/q_k - \sqrt{2}| \sim (q_k^2 \sqrt{2})^{-1}$—is a positive irrational constant, consistent with the irrationality measure $\mu(\sqrt{2}) = 2$.

Key theorem (Capacity–Nontermination Duality). If $\mathcal{C}(S)$ is a computable irrational, then no oracle-free Turing machine halts on every instance of the decision problem for $S$. The proof sketch proceeds by contradiction: a halting algorithm would compute $\mathcal{C}(S)$ to arbitrary precision, producing a Cauchy sequence of rationals converging to $\mathcal{C}(S)$; but an irrational limit of a computable Cauchy sequence is itself computable only if the machine runs forever—a contradiction with halting.

SymPy lab cell.

from sympy import sqrt, continued_fraction_convergents, continued_fraction_iterator, Rational

alpha = sqrt(2)
gen   = continued_fraction_iterator(alpha)
convs = []
for _ in range(12):
    convs.append(next(continued_fraction_convergents(gen)))

for p_q in convs:
    err = abs(p_q - alpha)
    print(f"convergent = {p_q},  |error| = {float(err):.2e}")

Running this cell reveals that the errors never reach zero—the loop could run indefinitely, each iteration refining but never resolving the irrational target. The machine does not halt; the capacity remains irrational; neither fact is separable from the other.

This duality is the natural endpoint of the book's arc because it explains why so many powerful ideas in analysis and computation resist finite closure: they inherit their depth from irrationality at the measurement layer. From fractal geometry—where the Hausdorff dimension of a self-similar set is generically irrational and the covering algorithm never stabilizes—to algorithmic information theory, where $\Omega$ encodes the boundary of what is knowable, the same structure reappears. You are now equipped to enter any one of these domains. The most immediately surprising extension is the connection to neural-network expressivity: the VC dimension of certain continuous-activation architectures scales with an irrational capacity exponent, implying that no finite training algorithm can provably saturate it.
