Subtraction Recurrence Trick

Definition. Let $\{a_n\}$ be a sequence satisfying a linear recurrence of the form

$$a_n = a_{n-1} - f(n), \quad n \geq 1,$$

where $f(n)$ is a known function of $n$ and $a_0$ is given. The subtraction recurrence trick is the technique of telescoping this recurrence—summing both sides over a range of indices—to express $a_n$ as a closed-form sum and, when possible, evaluate that sum in closed form. More precisely, writing

$$a_n - a_{n-1} = -f(n)$$

and summing from $k = 1$ to $k = n$ yields the telescoping identity

$$a_n = a_0 - \sum_{k=1}^{n} f(k).$$

The utility of this trick lies in reducing a recursive definition to a direct formula, provided the resulting sum $\sum_{k=1}^{n} f(k)$ is recognisable (e.g., arithmetic, geometric, or a standard closed form).

Worked Example. Define the sequence by $a_0 = 10$ and $a_n = a_{n-1} - \frac{1}{n(n+1)}$ for $n \geq 1$. Applying the telescoping identity:

$$a_n = 10 - \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{1}{k(k+1)}.$$

Using partial fractions, $\dfrac{1}{k(k+1)} = \dfrac{1}{k} - \dfrac{1}{k+1}$, so the sum telescopes further:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{1}{k(k+1)} = 1 - \frac{1}{n+1} = \frac{n}{n+1}.$$

Therefore $a_n = 10 - \dfrac{n}{n+1}$, a closed form requiring no iteration.

Key Theorem (Telescoping Sum). If $a_n - a_{n-1} = g(n)$ for all $n \geq 1$, then

$$a_n = a_0 + \sum_{k=1}^{n} g(k).$$

Proof sketch. Write out the $n$ equations $a_1 - a_0 = g(1),\ a_2 - a_1 = g(2),\ \ldots,\ a_n - a_{n-1} = g(n)$ and add them. Every intermediate term $a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_{n-1}$ cancels, leaving $a_n - a_0 = \sum_{k=1}^{n} g(k)$. $\square$

Lab Cell (SymPy).

from sympy import symbols, Sum, factor, simplify, Rational

n, k = symbols('n k', positive=True, integer=True)

f = 1 / (k * (k + 1))
closed_sum = Sum(f, (k, 1, n)).doit()   # telescopes symbolically
a_n = 10 - closed_sum
print("Closed form:", simplify(a_n))    # 10 - n/(n+1)

# Verify numerically for n = 5
a = Rational(10)
for i in range(1, 6):
    a -= Rational(1, i * (i + 1))
print("a_5 (iterative):", a)
print("a_5 (formula):  ", a_n.subs(n, 5))

Running this cell confirms that the symbolic closed form and the iterative computation agree, validating the trick on a concrete instance.
