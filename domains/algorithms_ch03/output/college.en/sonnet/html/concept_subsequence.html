Subsequence

Definition. Let $(a_n)_{n=1}^{\infty}$ be a sequence of real numbers. A subsequence of $(a_n)$ is a sequence of the form $(a_{n_k})_{k=1}^{\infty}$, where $(n_k)$ is a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers, i.e., $n_1 < n_2 < n_3 < \cdots$. In other words, a subsequence is obtained by selecting infinitely many terms from the original sequence while preserving their relative order.

Formally, $(a_{n_k})$ is indexed by the function $k \mapsto n_k$, which serves as a strictly increasing injection $\mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$. Every term $a_{n_k}$ is also a term of $(a_n)$, but not every term of $(a_n)$ need appear in the subsequence.

Worked Example. Consider the sequence $a_n = \frac{(-1)^n}{n}$, whose first several terms are $-1,\, \tfrac{1}{2},\, -\tfrac{1}{3},\, \tfrac{1}{4},\, -\tfrac{1}{5}, \ldots$ Define the index sequence $n_k = 2k$, so $n_1=2,\, n_2=4,\, n_3=6,\ldots$ The corresponding subsequence is

$$a_{n_k} = \frac{(-1)^{2k}}{2k} = \frac{1}{2k},$$

which is positive and decreasing, converging to $0$ as $k \to \infty$, even though the original sequence oscillates in sign.

Key Theorem (Bolzano–Weierstrass). Every bounded sequence of real numbers contains a convergent subsequence.

More precisely, if there exists $M > 0$ such that $|a_n| \leq M$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, then there exists a strictly increasing sequence of indices $(n_k)$ and a real number $L$ with $|L| \leq M$ such that $a_{n_k} \to L$ as $k \to \infty$.

Proof sketch. Partition $[-M, M]$ into two equal halves. At least one half contains infinitely many terms; select it and pick any term $a_{n_1}$ from it. Repeat the bisection on the chosen half, selecting $a_{n_2}$ with $n_2 > n_1$. The nested intervals have lengths $M \cdot 2^{1-k} \to 0$, so the selected terms form a Cauchy sequence, hence convergent in $\mathbb{R}$.

Lab Cell.

from sympy import Rational, oo, limit, symbols

k = symbols('k', positive=True, integer=True)

# Original sequence a_n = (-1)^n / n; extract even-indexed subsequence
subsequence = [Rational(1, 2*i) for i in range(1, 11)]
print("Subsequence terms:", subsequence)

# Symbolic limit of 1/(2k) as k -> oo
expr = Rational(1,1) / (2*k)
print("Limit:", limit(expr, k, oo))

Running this cell confirms that the subsequence $(1/2k)$ converges to $0$, illustrating both the definition and the analytic power of extracting a well-behaved subsequence from an oscillatory parent sequence.
