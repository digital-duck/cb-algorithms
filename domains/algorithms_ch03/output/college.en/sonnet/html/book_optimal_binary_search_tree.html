Concept Book: Optimal Binary Search Tree

Algorithms Ch03 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

What the concept achieves. Every decision process that retrieves information by key comparison is, at its mathematical core, a binary search tree (BST). The quality of that tree is measured by its expected search cost: if key $k_i$ is queried with probability $p_i$ and each unsuccessful search falls in gap $q_j$ with probability $q_j$, the expected number of comparisons is

$$ E[\text{cost}] = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (d_i + 1)\,p_i + \sum_{j=0}^{n} (d_j' + 1)\,q_j, $$

where $d_i$ is the depth of the $i$-th key node and $d_j'$ is the depth of the $j$-th failure leaf. An optimal BST minimises this quantity exactly — not heuristically. The Knuth–Yao dynamic-programming algorithm achieves this in $\Theta(n^2)$ time and $\Theta(n^2)$ space, exploiting the monotone root property: if $r(i,j)$ denotes the optimal root for keys $i$ through $j$, then $r(i,j-1) \le r(i,j) \le r(i+1,j)$. This monotonicity halves the inner loop and is the theorem that transforms an $O(n^3)$ recurrence into a tractable algorithm.

Why it is the natural endpoint. The concept-book has climbed steadily from raw tree definitions, through BST insertion and deletion, through cost models and recurrences, to this summit. Each prior chapter removed one degree of freedom — balancing by height (AVL), balancing by colour (red-black) — yet all height-balanced trees ignore access frequency. The optimal BST is the first structure that is cost-optimal under a known probability distribution, making it the convergence point of combinatorial structure and probabilistic analysis.

Worked example. Let $n = 3$ keys with $p_1 = 0.10,\ p_2 = 0.40,\ p_3 = 0.25$ and gap probabilities $q_0 = q_1 = q_2 = q_3 = 0.05$. Running the Knuth recurrence fills a $4\times 4$ cost table; the optimal root turns out to be $k_2$, with $k_1$ as its left child and $k_3$ as its right child, yielding $E[\text{cost}] = 2.10$ comparisons — versus $2.45$ for the naively balanced tree. The high-frequency key $k_2$ sits at the root and is found in one comparison; the saving compounds over millions of queries.
