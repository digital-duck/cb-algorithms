Brute Force Enumeration

Definition. Let $S$ be a finite set and $P : S \to \{\text{true}, \text{false}\}$ a decidable predicate. Brute force enumeration (also called exhaustive search) is the algorithm that evaluates $P(x)$ for every $x \in S$, collecting the solution set $\mathcal{F} = \{x \in S \mid P(x) = \text{true}\}$. No structural knowledge of $P$ is exploited; correctness is guaranteed by the completeness of the traversal. The time complexity is $\Theta(|S| \cdot T_P)$, where $T_P$ is the cost of a single predicate evaluation.

Worked Example. Find all integer pairs $(a, b)$ with $1 \le a \le b \le 20$ such that $a^2 + b^2$ is a perfect square (Pythagorean pairs).

Here $S = \{(a,b) \mid 1 \le a \le b \le 20\}$, so $|S| = \binom{20}{2} + 20 = 210$. The predicate is

$$P(a,b) \;=\; \Bigl[\sqrt{a^2 + b^2} \in \mathbb{Z}\Bigr].$$

Evaluating $P$ on all 210 pairs reveals exactly six solutions within the range: $(3,4)$, $(5,12)$, $(6,8)$, $(8,15)$, $(9,12)$, and $(12,16)$, corresponding to multiples of the primitive triples $(3,4,5)$ and $(5,12,13)$.

Key Theorem (Correctness of Exhaustive Search). If $S$ is finite and $P$ is computable, then brute force enumeration terminates and returns $\mathcal{F}$ exactly.

Proof sketch. Because $|S| < \infty$, the loop executes $|S|$ times and halts. At each iteration, $P(x)$ is computed and $x$ is included in $\mathcal{F}$ if and only if $P(x) = \text{true}$. Therefore $\mathcal{F} = \{x \in S \mid P(x)\}$ upon termination. $\blacksquare$

Brute force enumeration is optimal in the black-box model—when nothing beyond input-output behavior of $P$ is known—but quickly becomes impractical: a search space of size $2^{64}$ at $10^9$ evaluations per second would require roughly 585 years.

Lab Cell.

from sympy import sqrt, Integer

solutions = [
    (a, b)
    for a in range(1, 21)
    for b in range(a, 21)
    if sqrt(Integer(a**2 + b**2)).is_integer
]
print(solutions)

Running this cell reproduces all six Pythagorean pairs and illustrates how SymPy's exact arithmetic avoids floating-point rounding errors that would corrupt a naive math.sqrt test.
