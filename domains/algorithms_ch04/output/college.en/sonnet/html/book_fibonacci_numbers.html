Concept Book: Fibonacci Numbers

Algorithms Ch04 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

The Fibonacci sequence is defined by the recurrence

$$F_0 = 0,\quad F_1 = 1,\quad F_n = F_{n-1} + F_{n-2}\quad (n \geq 2),$$

producing the integers $0, 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, \ldots$ This deceptively simple rule — each term is the sum of the two preceding it — encodes some of the deepest regularities in mathematics and the natural world. Having built the algebraic and analytic machinery across the preceding chapters, you are now in a position to see why this sequence is not merely a curiosity but a structural endpoint: it sits at the intersection of linear recurrences, irrational arithmetic, combinatorics, and continuous dynamics.

Worked example. Compute $F_7$ from the closed-form expression (Binet's formula):

$$F_n = \frac{\varphi^n - \psi^n}{\sqrt{5}}, \qquad \varphi = \frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2},\quad \psi = \frac{1-\sqrt{5}}{2}.$$

For $n = 7$: $\varphi^7 \approx 29.034$ and $\psi^7 \approx -0.034$, so

$$F_7 = \frac{29.034 - (-0.034)}{\sqrt{5}} \approx \frac{29.068}{2.236} \approx 13.$$

The result is exact; the irrational parts cancel by construction.

Key theorem (growth rate). As $n \to \infty$, the ratio of consecutive Fibonacci numbers converges to the golden ratio:

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{F_{n+1}}{F_n} = \varphi \approx 1.61803\ldots$$

Proof sketch. Write $r = F_{n+1}/F_n$. Dividing the recurrence by $F_n$ gives $r = 1 + 1/r$, or $r^2 - r - 1 = 0$. The positive root is $\varphi$. Because all ratios are positive and bounded, the sequence of ratios converges, so its limit must satisfy this equation exactly. $\square$

This limit is the thread connecting Fibonacci numbers to a remarkable range of phenomena: the packing angles of sunflower seeds, the convergents of the continued fraction $[1;1,1,1,\ldots]$, the worst-case inputs for the Euclidean algorithm, and the spectral gaps of certain quasicrystal models.

Lab cell.

from sympy import fibonacci, sqrt, Rational, limit, Symbol, oo

n = Symbol('n', positive=True, integer=True)
# Verify Binet for n = 7
phi = (1 + sqrt(5)) / 2
psi = (1 - sqrt(5)) / 2
binet = (phi**7 - psi**7) / sqrt(5)
print("Binet F_7 =", binet.simplify())   # → 13

# Ratio convergence
ratios = [fibonacci(k+1) / fibonacci(k) for k in range(2, 20)]
print([float(r) for r in ratios])        # → approaching 1.618…

With this foundation in hand, you are ready to pursue one application in earnest. The most immediately accessible — and perhaps most surprising — is the Euclidean algorithm: the Fibonacci numbers are precisely the inputs that force the algorithm to take the maximum number of steps for a given digit length. Work through that connection next, and you will find that $\varphi$ is not decoration but the controlling constant behind the algorithm's worst-case complexity.
