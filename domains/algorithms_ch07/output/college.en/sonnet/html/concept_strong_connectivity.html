Strong Connectivity

Definition. Let $G = (V, E)$ be a directed graph (digraph). A vertex $v \in V$ is said to reach a vertex $u \in V$ if there exists a directed path from $v$ to $u$, i.e., a sequence $v = w_0, w_1, \ldots, w_k = u$ such that $(w_{i-1}, w_i) \in E$ for all $i \in \{1, \ldots, k\}$. The graph $G$ is strongly connected if every vertex reaches every other vertex: for all $u, v \in V$, $v$ reaches $u$ and $u$ reaches $v$. A strongly connected component (SCC) of $G$ is a maximal strongly connected subgraph; every digraph partitions uniquely into its SCCs.

Worked Example. Consider the digraph on vertices $V = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ with edges $E = \{(1,2),(2,3),(3,1),(3,4)\}$. Starting from vertex 1, we reach 2 via $(1,2)$, then 3 via $(2,3)$, then back to 1 via $(3,1)$: the cycle $\{1,2,3\}$ is mutually reachable. Vertex 4 is reachable from every member of $\{1,2,3\}$ through vertex 3, but no edge leads back into $\{1,2,3\}$ from 4. Therefore the graph has two SCCs: $\{1,2,3\}$ and $\{4\}$. Contracting each SCC to a single node yields the condensation $G^{\mathrm{SCC}}$, a directed acyclic graph (DAG).

Theorem (Kosaraju, 1978). The SCCs of a digraph $G$ on $n$ vertices and $m$ edges can be identified in $\Theta(n + m)$ time by two depth-first searches: one on $G$ and one on the transpose $G^T$ (all edge directions reversed), processing vertices in decreasing finish-time order from the first search.

Proof sketch. After the first DFS on $G$, the vertex with the largest finish time lies in a source SCC of the condensation. Running DFS from that vertex on $G^T$ visits exactly its SCC, because edges in $G^T$ that would escape to another SCC point into source SCCs of $G^T$, which correspond to sink SCCs of $G$ and are therefore unreachable. Repeating the argument on the residual graph completes the induction. $\square$

Lab Cell.

import sympy as sp
from sympy.combinatorics.permutations import Permutation
import networkx as nx          # standard companion to SymPy for graphs

G = nx.DiGraph()
G.add_edges_from([(1,2),(2,3),(3,1),(3,4)])

sccs = list(nx.strongly_connected_components(G))
print("SCCs:", sccs)                       # [{1,2,3}, {4}]

condensation = nx.condensation(G, scc=sccs)
print("Condensation edges:", list(condensation.edges()))  # [(0,1)] — a DAG

Running this cell confirms the two SCCs found analytically and that the condensation is acyclic, illustrating the theorem concretely before tackling larger instances.
