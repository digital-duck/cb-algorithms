Choosing Reduction Source

Definition. Let $F = \{f_1, f_2, \ldots, f_s\} \subset k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ be a finite set of polynomials over a field $k$, and let $p$ be a polynomial to be reduced. A reduction step replaces $p$ by $p - c \cdot x^\alpha f_i$ whenever the leading monomial $\mathrm{LM}(f_i)$ divides some monomial $x^\alpha$ appearing in $p$, and $c \in k$ is the appropriate scalar. The reduction source is the element $f_i \in F$ chosen to perform a given step. When more than one $f_i$ simultaneously divides some monomial of $p$, the algorithm must select exactly one source; this selection is the reduction source choice.

Worked Example. Let $k = \mathbb{Q}$, with graded lexicographic order $x > y$, and set $F = \{f_1, f_2\}$ where

$$f_1 = xy - 1, \qquad f_2 = y^2 - 1.$$

Reduce $p = xy^2 + y$. The leading term of $p$ is $xy^2$.

  • Choice A — use $f_1$: $\mathrm{LM}(f_1) = xy$ divides $xy^2$, so subtract $y \cdot f_1 = xy^2 - y$. This gives $p \leftarrow y + y = 2y$, which is already reduced with remainder $2y$.
  • Choice B — use $f_2$: $\mathrm{LM}(f_2) = y^2$ divides $xy^2$, so subtract $x \cdot f_2 = xy^2 - x$. This gives $p \leftarrow x + y$, which requires a further step with $f_1$ to reduce the $xy$ term if present; here no monomial of $x + y$ is divisible by $\mathrm{LM}(f_1)$ or $\mathrm{LM}(f_2)$, so the remainder is $x + y$.

    • The two choices yield distinct remainders, $2y$ versus $x + y$. This illustrates that the remainder from polynomial division is source-order-dependent for an arbitrary generating set.

    Key Theorem (Uniqueness via Gröbner Bases). Let $G$ be a Gröbner basis for the ideal $I = \langle F \rangle$ with respect to a fixed monomial order. Then for any polynomial $p$, the remainder $\bar{p}^G$ obtained by reducing $p$ modulo $G$ is unique, regardless of the reduction source choices made during the algorithm. In particular, $p \in I$ if and only if $\bar{p}^G = 0$. The proof follows from the property that every common multiple of leading monomials in $G$ has an S-polynomial that reduces to zero, eliminating the ambiguity present in arbitrary generating sets.

    Lab Cell.

    from sympy import symbols, groebner, Poly, GF
from sympy.polys.orderings import grlex

x, y = symbols('x y')
F = [x*y - 1, y**2 - 1]
G = groebner(F, x, y, order='grlex')
p = x*y**2 + y

# Reduce p modulo the Gröbner basis
remainder = Poly(p, x, y, order='grlex').rem(
    [Poly(g, x, y, order='grlex') for g in G]
)
print("Gröbner basis:", list(G))
print("Unique remainder:", remainder)

    Running this cell confirms that $G$ yields a unique remainder independent of source selection, concretely demonstrating the theorem above.
