Min Plus Matrix Multiplication

Definition. Let $A = (a_{ij})$ and $B = (b_{ij})$ be $n \times n$ matrices with entries in $\mathbb{R} \cup \{+\infty\}$. The min-plus product (also called the tropical product) of $A$ and $B$ is the matrix $C = A \otimes B$ defined entry-wise by

$$ c_{ij} = \bigoplus_{k=1}^{n} (a_{ik} \odot b_{kj}) = \min_{1 \le k \le n}(a_{ik} + b_{kj}). $$

Here the tropical operations $\odot$ (addition) and $\oplus$ (minimum) replace classical multiplication and addition, respectively. The value $+\infty$ acts as the additive identity ($\oplus$-identity), mirroring the role of $0$ in conventional arithmetic.

Worked Example. Consider the $3 \times 3$ matrices

$$ A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 3 & +\infty \\ 2 & 0 & 1 \\ +\infty & 4 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad B = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 5 \\ 2 & 0 & 3 \\ 4 & 2 & 0 \end{pmatrix}. $$

To compute $c_{13}$ (row 1, column 3 of $C = A \otimes B$):

$$ c_{13} = \min(a_{11}+b_{13},\; a_{12}+b_{23},\; a_{13}+b_{33}) = \min(0+5,\; 3+3,\; +\infty+0) = \min(5, 6, +\infty) = 5. $$

Repeating for all entries yields $C$, which encodes the shortest path lengths between all pairs of nodes when $A$ and $B$ represent weighted adjacency matrices of a directed graph.

Key Theorem (Repeated Squaring for All-Pairs Shortest Paths). Let $W$ be the $n \times n$ weighted adjacency matrix of a graph (with $w_{ii} = 0$ and $w_{ij} = +\infty$ if no edge exists). Then the $(i,j)$ entry of the $k$-fold min-plus power $W^{\otimes k}$ equals the length of the shortest path from $i$ to $j$ using at most $k$ edges. Consequently, $W^{\otimes (n-1)}$ gives the all-pairs shortest path matrix, computable in $O(n^3 \log n)$ time via repeated squaring.

Lab Cell (SymPy / Python).

import numpy as np

def min_plus(A, B):
    n = A.shape[0]
    C = np.full((n, n), np.inf)
    for i in range(n):
        for j in range(n):
            C[i, j] = np.min(A[i, :] + B[:, j])
    return C

W = np.array([[0, 3, np.inf],
              [2, 0,      1],
              [np.inf, 4, 0]], dtype=float)

print(min_plus(W, W))   # shortest paths of length ≤ 2
